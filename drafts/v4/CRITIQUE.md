# Relax v4 — critique (3 drafts vs the spec)

Spec: ~400 code points (380–430), 4 paragraphs, English, ONE complete slowing
motion, ONE trackable visual anchor, ending in a concrete visual image of
total stillness, a human craft element if it fits, sentences that shorten as
the motion slows. No pottery, pendulum, or spinning top.

All three drafts are in range on length and paragraph count:
- draft-1-loom: 407 code points, 4 paragraphs (118, 128, 81, 74)
- draft-2-brush: 385 code points, 4 paragraphs (112, 97, 89, 81)
- draft-3-oven: 408 code points, 4 paragraphs (116, 112, 85, 89)

## Draft 1 — weaver's loom (407)

- Brevity: in range, but the longest of the three in paragraph 2 (128).
- Anchor: the shuttle crossing the warp. A recurring beat — it crosses,
  crosses again slower, crosses once more slower still, then stops mid-warp.
  Very trackable, and the closest structural sibling to the winner's
  returning-notch mechanism. Strong.
- Stillness ending: the shuttle lying still between the threads, the cloth
  waiting in the light. Concrete and visual. Strong.
- Craft: the weaver is present throughout (foot, hands, wool). Strong.
- Shortening sentences: yes — paragraph 1 sentences average ~59 chars,
  paragraph 4 ~37.
- Weaknesses: the shuttle was our partner's runner-up candidate, so this
  scene is the least distinct of the three. "Beater" is slightly technical
  vocabulary. The mechanism is the most derivative of the winner's — it
  replays the returning-beat shape rather than finding a new one.

## Draft 2 — calligraphy brush (385)

- Brevity: the shortest and most focused of the three (385), closest to the
  winner's discipline of one image, one anchor, minimal distraction.
- Anchor: the brush tip moving along the stroke. The reader follows the tip
  with their eyes — and the slowing is visible in the artifact itself: the
  line thins as the ink runs low and the brush slows. The trace records the
  slowing. No other draft makes the deceleration itself visible. Strongest
  anchor on this criterion.
- Stillness ending: the brush lying still on its rest, tip dark with ink,
  the character drying. Concrete, visual, total. Strong.
- Craft: the calligrapher is present in every paragraph through the brush,
  the ink, and the paper. Strong.
- Shortening sentences: the cleanest arc of the three — paragraphs open
  around 55 chars per sentence and close around 20–35.
- Distinctness: the most distinct from our partner's earlier candidates.
- Weaknesses: the anchor is a single progressing stroke rather than a
  returning beat, so it leans less on the proven returning-anchor shape.
  The "once more" beat is shared with the loom draft's grammar.

## Draft 3 — bread oven (408)

- Brevity: in range.
- Anchor: the flame. It does not return and cannot be tracked — it shrinks
  and fades. The slowing motion (a flame moving along a log) is also the
  least legible of the three; flames lick, they do not travel slowly.
  Weakest anchor by a clear margin.
- Stillness ending: the loaf lying still on the stone, crust set and
  gleaming. Concrete and visual — a good ending, but the anchor never
  earns it.
- Craft: the baker is present in paragraph 1 and then vanishes; the fire
  takes over the piece. Weakest craft element.
- Shortening sentences: partial — the longest sentence sits mid-piece in
  paragraph 2.
- Weaknesses: anchor and motion both fail the spec's core test. Also
  adjacent to our partner's bread-dough candidate.

## Decision

Winner: **draft-2-brush (calligraphy brush)**.

It is the shortest and most focused; its anchor is visual and trackable in
a way no other draft achieves, because the slowing is visible in the line
itself as it thins; its ending is a concrete visual image of total
stillness; the craft element runs through every paragraph; its sentences
shorten cleanly; and it is the most distinct from our partner's candidates.
The loom is a close runner-up on the strength of its recurring shuttle
beat, but it is more derivative of the winner's mechanism and less
distinct. The oven is third — its anchor does not track.
